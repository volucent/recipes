# Ninja Creami XL — Recipe Context

Every recipe in this folder is built for the **Ninja Creami XL (Deluxe, 24 oz pints)**. These are not generic ice cream recipes: the machine shaves a solid frozen block, so the base, freeze, and spin all have to suit it.

Source: Exercise4CheatMeals, ["3 years of CREAMi advice in 25 minutes"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TD0CWbYfy0U).

## Machine

- **Settings differ by model.** Programs with the same name spin at different speeds and times on the original, Deluxe/XL, and Swirl. Most online recipes are developed on the original 16 oz model, so expect about 90% of the result and adjust.
- **Don't trust setting labels.** Some programs are basically clones. Test a new base on 2–3 settings (e.g. Lite Ice Cream vs Ice Cream) and record the winner in the recipe.
- **Scale 16 oz recipes ×1.5** for the 24 oz XL pint.
- **Never freeze a near-water base.** A solid ice brick strains the machine.

## Base

- **High-protein milk (e.g. Fairlife) beats regular milk.** It has less water and more casein, so it's smoother and less icy. 1% milk works if xanthan gum and salt are included. Almond milk is icy, so only use it mixed with dairy.
- **Frozen tastes less sweet.** A single-ingredient pint (plain chocolate milk, a protein shake) comes out bland and icy. Always add flavor (cocoa), sweetener, xanthan gum, and salt.
- **Salt in every recipe.**
- **Keep some fat**, 40–100 kcal per pint (cream or an egg yolk). A fat-free pint tastes flat. Egg yolk is a strong emulsifier and adds creaminess.
- **Sweeteners change texture and freezing point**, not just taste, so swapping one changes the pint. Erythritol tastes the most like sugar and causes the fewest gut issues. A small amount of real sugar (~10 g / 40 kcal) cuts sweetener aftertaste and ice crystals.
- **Protein powder must taste good on its own**, or the pint is ruined. It adds creaminess and reduces iciness. Whey–casein blends over-thicken: max ½ scoop. Too much protein makes a frozen protein shake, not ice cream.
- **Xanthan gum is the stabilizer**, the winner of blind tests against the others. Use about 0.5 g per 16 oz pint (~0.75 g for the XL) and weigh it; too much makes it gummy.
- **Avoid:** cottage cheese bases (the creator's verdict: they taste bad), pudding mix (artificial flavor), imitation vanilla.
- **Extracts vary a lot by brand.** Start small. Peppermint masks bad-tasting protein powder.

## Prep

- **Weigh ingredients in grams.** Small amounts like xanthan gum need a scale.
- **Mix with an immersion blender, not a frother.** Keep blending ~90 s after it looks dissolved.
- **Fill the liquid to the MAX FILL line, not past it.** Foam above the line is fine; it rises and compresses when frozen.
- **Prevent the frozen hump** in the middle, which can damage the machine. Either rest the mix 15 min after blending, or chill it in the fridge 3–4 hr first. Then freeze **lid off**. If a hump still forms, melt it level with a hot, damp paper towel.
- **Freeze 24 hr minimum.** Heated or cooked bases need ~36 hr. A pint that melts after one spin wasn't frozen enough.

## Spin

- **Icy sides:** run hot water over the outside of the pint for 1 min before spinning.
- **Finishing spin:** if the pint is 90–95% smooth, one run on **Mix-In** finishes it.
- **Re-spun leftovers never match a fresh spin.**

## Mix-ins

- The Mix-In program pulverizes soft mix-ins.
- **Chocolate:** melt it with a little oil, then freeze. Plain frozen chips taste waxy.
- **Soft mix-ins** (cookies, bars): freeze them first. Dip cookies in milk, then freeze.
- Make the hole **off-center or in a corner**. Add the mix-in fast and get the pint back into the machine before it melts.

## Cleaning

- Wash the lid, paddle, and outer bowl lid after **every** use. Ice cream trapped in the lid ferments and molds.

## Recipe format

Each recipe in this folder states:
- Ingredients with grams (cups are optional extras)
- The setting it was tested on
- Freeze time
- Spin sequence (setting → re-spin/Mix-In as needed)
